Potato refers to a root vegetable. It can also refer to weak computational hardware, like the one I'm writing this blog on, and the one you're reading it on. "Weak" is a relative term. In this context, we're referring to anything (with a GPU side) that is weak in comparison to an NVIDIA H100/200.

The fanless and furious, despite working with potatoes, want to have an option to host LLMs locally. Folks at llama.cpp, vLLM and SGLang have very thankfully made a lot of this possible with their inference engines. Likewise, many organizations like Deepseek, Meta, Google, Z.ai, HuggingFace have made possible for us to have something to host with these inference engines.

With many crazy good open source models coming to the market, we're left with a seemingly unanswerable question - which one should I host and use locally? This decision is a function of a few factors, with the primary ones being:
1. What's the max that my system can handle?
2. What's the best intelligence I can get when subject to constraint #1?
3. What does intelligence mean for me?

After a break of five years, I'm back to creating tools for fun and whimsy. This time, I happen to have earned some adult money, and bought a domain [potatoinference.io](https://potatoinference.io) to help the fanless and furious (including myself) answer the questions above. Simply, given your system constraints, and your definition of intelligence, which model(s) are the best for you.
